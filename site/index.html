How options work here

Each option expires at the end of the month (21 trading days) and settles in cash. Selling at the bid writes the option; buying at the ask buys it. Written calls must be covered by shares you hold, and written puts need cash equal to the strike value. Implied vol rises for lower strikes: puts are priced richer than calls. Calls below 100% and puts above 100% are in the money; their prices are shaded.

      How it works

      A trading game on real daily prices, scored the way a portfolio manager is judged.

      A turn Rules Option pricing Scoring Benchmarks Challenges Data Limits

      A turn

      Rules

      • Stake. $100,000, fixed. No deposits or withdrawals.
      • The stock. Drawn at random from a pool with very different histories, including the S&P 500 itself. In blind mode the name and dates are hidden and the price starts at 100; returns are unchanged.
      • Window. The full challenge is the most recent 60 months (1,260 trading days) ending at the latest close. A quick round is a 12-month slice. Each turn is 21 trading days.
      • Stock. A percent of equity, from −100% (short) to +150% (long on margin). Each trade costs 0.05% of the amount traded. Leave it alone and your share count stays the same.
      • Options. European calls and puts at 90, 95, 100, 105 and 110% of the price, expiring at the end of the month and settled in cash. Size is a percent of equity. Up to four a month, 200% of equity at most in total.
      • Leverage includes options. Net exposure, the stock plus each option's delta, must stay between −100% and +150% when you place an order.
      • Writing options. Written calls must be covered by shares. Written puts need cash equal to the strike value after the whole order, not counting short-sale proceeds.
      • Financing. Cash earns the 13-week T-bill rate. Borrowing pays T-bill + 1.5%, shorts pay 0.5% a year, and an order can't take borrowing above 50% of equity.
      • Drift and margin. Limits apply to orders; between orders positions drift. If equity falls below 25% of the gross stock position, a margin call sells everything. At zero equity the game ends and the account ranks last.

      Option pricing

      There are no free historical option quotes for every stock, so options are priced with Black-Scholes on an implied volatility built from that day's market data:

      IVatm² = RV63(stock)² + [ (0.88 × VIX/100)² − RV63(S&P 500)² ]

      IV(K) = IVatm × (1 − slope × ln(K/S))

      The bracket is the market's volatility risk premium that day. Adding it once prices at-the-money S&P 500 options at 0.88 × VIX and gives single stocks a small premium over their own realized volatility. A smile then makes lower strikes richer.

      The details

      RV63 is trailing 63-day realized volatility. The VIX is a variance-swap level that includes the skew, so at-the-money index volatility is taken as 0.88 × VIX. The index smile has slope 3.0 on both sides; single stocks get 1.25 below the money and a flat call wing, because out-of-the-money stock calls aren't cheap in real markets. Each smile is clipped to between 0.5 and 2 times at-the-money volatility; at-the-money volatility is floored at 75% of realized; every IV stays between 8% and 250%. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid: a half-spread of 2.5% of the premium, at least 0.05% of the stock price. The risk-free rate is the T-bill yield, and because prices include dividends, the stock is treated as paying none.

      Three earlier versions were replaced after reality checks on real data: one made covered calls beat buy and hold on almost every stock; one let a 5%-out-of-the-money S&P 500 covered call beat the index by 46 points; one made out-of-the-money stock calls so cheap that call buyers profited even after delta hedging. With this version, delta-hedged buyers of out-of-the-money stock options lose on average, as the evidence says they should, and the S&P 500 buy-write finishes within about 5 points of the index (+83% vs +78%).

      Scoring

      • Sharpe ratio. Mean daily return above the T-bill rate, divided by its standard deviation, × √252.
      • Maximum drawdown. The largest fall from a peak in the daily account value.
      • IRR. The money-weighted return per year (XIRR). With a fixed stake it equals the compound annual growth rate.
      • Return per unit of drawdown. IRR above T-bills divided by max drawdown (floored at 1%).
      • Overall place. Each strategy is ranked on Sharpe, return per unit of drawdown, and IRR, and ordered by the average rank; equal averages go to the higher Sharpe, then the higher IRR, and only exact ties share a place. Drawdown enters per unit of return, so a tiny position can't win just by being small. A wiped-out account ranks last.
      • Also shown. Sortino ratio (excess return over downside deviation), volatility, and beta against the S&P 500.

      Benchmarks

      Six strategies play the same window with the same costs: buy and hold the stock; buy and hold the S&P 500; a covered call (long 100%, write 105% calls monthly); a protective put (long 100%, buy 95% puts monthly); a 200-day trend rule (long above the 200-day average, otherwise T-bills); and T-bills only.

      Head-to-head challenges

      After a game, Challenge a friend copies a link that holds your stock, your window and every order you placed, so no server or login is involved. Your friend plays the same blind tape from month 1 without seeing your result. When they finish, the engine replays your orders on the same prices and ranks you side by side, with the six benchmarks. They can challenge you back.

      Data and updates

      Daily dividend- and split-adjusted closes for every stock, the S&P 500 ETF, the VIX and the 13-week T-bill yield are downloaded by a scheduled GitHub Actions job each weekday after the close. It validates every series and commits only real changes, and the site redeploys. The game window always ends at the latest close. Adding a stock is one line in config/tickers.json. See the Data page for current status.

      The engine has unit tests (Black-Scholes against textbook values and put-call parity, margin calls, settlement, the trading rules and every metric), and a separate pandas script recomputes every metric on real data and checks it against the game engine's own numbers.

      Limits

      • Option prices are model prices, not historical quotes; the smile is a simple shape and earnings days aren't modeled.
      • Trades fill at the daily close, decisions are monthly, fractional shares are allowed, and market impact is ignored beyond the fixed costs.
      • Blind mode is honor-based: a careful look at the chart's shape or the T-bill rate can hint at the stock or era, the browser's developer tools can show the price file, and a challenge or replay link can be decoded to show the stock.
      • Replay links reproduce a game exactly on the same data. If the provider revises adjusted prices, a replay can differ by a few dollars.

      How updates work

      A GitHub Actions workflow (.github/workflows/update-data.yml) runs every weekday at 22:30 UTC, after the US market close, when the config changes, or on demand. It tries yfinance, then the Yahoo Finance chart API, then Nasdaq's historical API (and FRED for T-bills), validates every series, and commits only changed files; the site redeploys from that commit. If a source fails, the last good data is kept and this page flags it as stale.
